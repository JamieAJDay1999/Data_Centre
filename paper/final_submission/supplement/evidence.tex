\section{UPS investment-screen calculations}
Let the additional annual electricity-cost saving for energy capacity $E$ be $\Delta S(E)=C^{\op}(600)-C^{\op}(E)$. The retained results give GBP~1,292.2794/year for $E=750$ kWh and GBP~2,481.4101/year for $E=900$ kWh. Relative to the central capacity, the additional nameplate energies are 150 and 300 kWh, but the additional energy between operational bounds is 75 and 150 kWh because the 50\% reserve fraction remains fixed.

For constant real annual benefits, additional investment $I$ and additional annual non-electricity costs $O$, the screen is
\begin{equation}
\Delta S-O-I\,\mathrm{CRF}(r,n)\geq0,\qquad
\mathrm{CRF}(r,n)=\frac{r(1+r)^n}{(1+r)^n-1}.
\end{equation}
At $r=0$, CRF is $1/n$. The capital recovery factor converts initial expenditure into an equivalent annual amount~\cite{sam_crf}. Setting $O=0$ gives $I_{\mathrm{BE}}=\Delta S/\mathrm{CRF}$. Dividing by added \emph{nameplate} kWh gives a threshold directly comparable with a cost expressed on that same basis. The ten-year, 8\% case has CRF 0.14902949. Table~\ref{tab:financial-range} shows additional illustrative rate/life combinations; these are assumptions for interpreting the result, not empirical estimates of project finance or battery life.

\input{tables/financial_range.tex}

No battery market price is assumed, so the calculation establishes a maximum supported expenditure rather than a positive investment recommendation. Comparing a vendor quotation would require its currency, price year, chemistry, installed scope, integration work, reserve policy and usable/nameplate capacity convention. Existing power electronics are held fixed in the dispatch sensitivity; an upgrade requiring additional converter capacity cannot be evaluated by changing only a battery price per kWh.

Losses already enter the dispatch costs through efficiency and must not be charged twice. Degradation, additional O\&M, replacements and financing/tax detail are omitted. Introducing additional costs reduces supported investment under the same annual-benefit assumption. A more complete lifetime treatment could also change the benefit itself. In particular, initial UPS energy is proportional to capacity and terminal energy has no credit. The retained annual cases finish with 300, 375 and 450 kWh for 600, 750 and 900 kWh capacity, respectively. The resulting single-year differences are not independently established steady-state annual benefits. The earlier central terminal check in S7 does not resolve this for all enlarged-capacity cases.

Accordingly, repeating the 2025 incremental saving over $n$ years is an explicit conditional assumption. The thresholds are gross screens before omitted costs, not forecasts of a site's investment return. They exclude the possible value of greater backup provision and any revenues from grid services. Neither the event heatmap nor the annual component accounting supplies those revenues. If credible installed incremental costs exceed the stated threshold, the assumed energy-arbitrage saving alone is insufficient; if they are lower, the screen is favourable only within the stated operational and financial assumptions.

\section{Methodology}\label{sec:methodology}

\subsection{Rolling-horizon formulation}

The data centre is modelled at 15-minute resolution as an integrated mixed-integer
linear programme (MILP). Each local calendar day forms the committed core of one
solve and is followed by a three-hour look-ahead. The core therefore contains
92, 96 or 100 intervals at the daylight-saving transitions, while the look-ahead
always contains 12 intervals. Only core intervals are included in the reported
calendar-year cost. The look-ahead establishes a feasible continuation for
workload and physical states.

The state at the end of a committed core,
\begin{equation}
 \boldsymbol{x}_{d}^{\mathrm{end}} =
 \left(E^{\mathrm{UPS}},E^{\mathrm{TES}},T_{\mathrm{IT}},
 T_{\mathrm{R}},T_{\mathrm{CA}},T_{\mathrm{HA}}\right),
\end{equation}
is imposed exactly as the initial state of day $d+1$. Outstanding flexible
workload is passed in the same way as timestamped cohorts with absolute
deadlines. This linking prevents the artificial daily reset of stored energy,
temperature or deferred work.

\subsection{IT workload and electrical power}

The workload trace specifies inflexible CPU utilisation and four flexible
tranches whose maximum delays are 0.5, 1, 2 and 3 hours. If $a_{c}$ is the
CPU-hours in cohort $c$ and $\mathcal{W}_{c}$ is its feasible execution window,
the scheduled fraction $u_{c,t}$ satisfies
\begin{align}
 \sum_{t\in\mathcal{W}_{c}} u_{c,t}\Delta t &= a_c,\\
 u_t^{\mathrm{inflex}}+\sum_c u_{c,t} &\leq 1.
\end{align}
The first equation is enforced within the available horizon when the deadline
is visible; otherwise the unexecuted balance is carried forward. The complete
three-hour look-ahead is long enough to expose every deadline generated in a
core.

IT power is a convex nonlinear function of aggregate CPU utilisation in the
source equipment model. It is represented by a four-segment non-uniform DLOG
piecewise-linear approximation. Across the feasible utilisation range, the
maximum approximation error is 6.241 kW, or 0.624\% of the 1 MW IT rating.

\subsection{UPS, cooling and thermal states}

The UPS energy recursion is
\begin{equation}
 E^{\mathrm{UPS}}_{t+1}=E^{\mathrm{UPS}}_t+
 \eta_{\mathrm{ch}}P^{\mathrm{UPS}}_{\mathrm{ch},t}\Delta t-
 \frac{P^{\mathrm{UPS}}_{\mathrm{dis},t}}{\eta_{\mathrm{dis}}}\Delta t .
\end{equation}
Binary mode variables prevent simultaneous charging and discharging. The
central UPS has a 600 kWh energy capacity and a 50\% lower state-of-charge
bound. The first annual interval starts at 600 kWh; thereafter no daily cyclic
condition is imposed.

The chiller supplies the CRAC directly and can also charge the 1000 kWh-th TES.
The TES may discharge to the CRAC, with mutually exclusive charge and discharge
modes. TES begins at 500 kWh-th and uses 0.9 charge and discharge efficiencies.
The four-node facility thermal model is discretised with backward
(implicit) Euler boundary states. Both benchmark and optimised cases use the
same $18$--$23\,^{\circ}$C cold-aisle range. A conservative operating rule,
\begin{equation}
 Q^{\mathrm{cool}}_t \geq P^{\mathrm{IT}}_t ,
\end{equation}
requires contemporaneous cooling provision to be at least the IT heat load.
This avoids treating unvalidated heat accumulation as a flexibility resource.
Outdoor temperature is fixed at $22\,^{\circ}$C and the chiller coefficient of
performance at 5 throughout the stylised annual experiment.

Total grid import is
\begin{equation}
 P^{\mathrm{grid}}_t=P^{\mathrm{grid,IT}}_t+
 P^{\mathrm{UPS}}_{\mathrm{ch},t}+P^{\mathrm{ch,CRAC}}_t+
 P^{\mathrm{ch,TES}}_t+P^{\mathrm{OD}},
\end{equation}
where $P^{\mathrm{OD}}$ is the constant auxiliary load. The objective minimises
the signed settlement cost over the complete core and look-ahead:
\begin{equation}
 \min \sum_t \pi_t P^{\mathrm{grid}}_t\Delta t/1000 .
\end{equation}
Negative prices are retained. The central model assigns no storage degradation
cost, standing loss, additional UPS reserve or terminal-energy credit.

\subsection{Annual cases and sensitivity design}

The annual benchmark executes workload at arrival and disables UPS and TES
dispatch, while retaining cost-optimised cooling within the same temperature
bounds. The optimised case activates flexible workload scheduling and both
storage systems. This is a comparison of operational flexibility with the
implemented benchmark; it is not a comparison against a fixed $22.5\,^{\circ}$C
cooling set point.

The input price is the actual signed 2025 Intermittent Market Reference Price
(IMRP) series, expanded from hourly to quarter-hour resolution. The local-clock
workload profile repeats daily, while an absolute UTC timeline preserves both
daylight-saving transitions. Pyomo and HiGHS are used with a requested relative
MILP gap of 0.1\%, a 300-second daily limit and a normal accepted gap of 1\%.

Full-year one-at-a-time endpoints vary flexible-workload share, UPS energy
capacity and TES energy capacity to $0.5\times$ and $1.5\times$ their central
values. Storage power ratings are held fixed and starting stored-energy
fractions are preserved. Twelve representative weeks provide the intermediate
$0.75\times$ and $1.25\times$ response shape. Because the sampled central saving
differs from the annual saving, sampled curves are centred on their own central
case and are not reported as annual estimates.

\subsection{Representative-day flexibility assessment}\label{sec:scenario 3}

The duration-aware flexibility formulation evaluates a request beginning at
$t_0$ with grid deviation $\Delta P$ from the annual optimised baseline. During
the event,
\begin{equation}
 \left|P^{\mathrm{grid}}_t-
 \left(P^{\mathrm{grid,base}}_t+\Delta P\right)\right|
 \leq \epsilon_P ,
\end{equation}
and a binary search finds the longest feasible duration. Recovery constraints
require the terminal UPS, TES and four thermal states to return to the baseline
continuation state. An objective representative day is selected by the smallest
standardised distance to annual price, grid, CPU, cost and opening-state
statistics; this procedure selects 22 September 2025.

\section{Annual Results and Discussion}\label{sec:results}

\subsection{Central annual result}

The workload-at-arrival benchmark costs GBP~550,503.59 in 2025, whereas the
integrated optimisation costs GBP~522,707.23. The resulting saving is
GBP~27,796.37, or 5.049\%. Table~\ref{tab:annual-endpoints} gives the central
case and all completed full-year sensitivity endpoints.

The optimised case imports 6.794 GWh compared with 6.718 GWh in the benchmark.
The economic benefit therefore arises from moving electrical consumption to
lower-priced intervals, not from reducing annual electricity use. The 1 MW
facility's annual peak import rises from 974.9 kW to 1681.9 kW as storage
charging and deferred-work recovery are concentrated in favourable intervals.
Consequently, the result should be interpreted as an energy-market operating
cost benefit; network-capacity charges or an explicit grid-import limit would
change the dispatch.

\subsection{Sensitivity and asset ranking}

Flexible-workload share is the dominant tested internal parameter. Halving it
reduces the annual saving to 3.522\%, while increasing it by half raises the
saving to 6.257\%. The corresponding changes from the central result are
$-1.528$ and $+1.208$ percentage points. UPS capacity ranks second: its endpoint
range is 4.565--5.501\%. TES capacity has the smallest effect, with a
4.800--5.167\% range. Thus the frozen ranking is flexible workload, UPS energy
capacity, then TES energy capacity.

\subsection{Continuity, solver quality and terminal treatment}

Every annual chain passes timestamp coverage, state hand-off, workload
conservation, charge/discharge exclusivity and settlement-cost reconciliation
audits. The few time-limited daily solves retain feasible incumbents. The
largest exceptional relative gap occurs in the low-flexibility case on 25 May:
a 900-second rerun leaves a 5.652\% relative gap because the objective is close
to zero and negative, but the absolute incumbent-bound width is only GBP~0.102.
The committed cost changes by approximately GBP~0.002, the downstream state is
unchanged and every physical audit passes. It is retained with this explicit
qualification.

At midnight after 31 December, 0.642875 CPU-hours from the central core remains
within deadline. It is fully completed in the 1 January 2026 look-ahead, which
uses the available actual IMRP values of GBP~46.49, 40.19 and 28.99/MWh for the
first three settlement periods. Calendar-year reporting includes only 2025
committed intervals; the look-ahead is a feasibility certificate and includes
ordinary 2026 demand that cannot be attributed to 2025.

The central convention assigns zero value to terminal UPS and TES energy. A
continuation-value test spanning the median to the maximum observed 1 January
price changes the annual cost by at most 0.000381\%. At the maximum tested
value, the final horizon retains 600 kWh in the UPS and 1000 kWh-th in TES
instead of ending at their lower states. This negligible annual effect supports
the zero-value central convention while making its implication transparent.

\subsection{Scope and limitations}

The analysis is a stylised annual operating experiment rather than a forecast
for a specific facility. Ambient temperature, COP and the local-clock workload
shape are fixed, storage ageing and standing losses are omitted, and network
tariffs and service revenues are outside scope. The conservative
$Q^{\mathrm{cool}}\geq P^{\mathrm{IT}}$ rule also limits the use of building
thermal mass. The cost results establish the value and relative importance of
the modelled resources under signed energy prices; they do not alone establish
the profitability of a commercial flexibility product.

\section{Conclusion}

Linked rolling optimisation across all of 2025 reduces the model data centre's
electricity cost from GBP~550,503.59 to GBP~522,707.23, a saving of 5.049\%.
The calculation preserves physical and workload continuity across every local
day, including daylight-saving transitions, and reconciles directly to signed
IMRP settlement prices.

Full-year endpoints show that workload flexibility is the most influential
tested internal resource, followed by UPS energy capacity and then TES energy
capacity. Representative-week intermediate points preserve this ranking and
response shape but overstate the absolute annual effect, so they are used only
as centred comparative evidence. Year-end continuation-value tests alter
annual cost by no more than 0.000381\%, and all 0.642875 CPU-hours outstanding
at midnight are completed within deadline in the actual 1 January look-ahead.

The next revision will apply the prepared duration-aware flexibility runner to
22 September 2025 using the exact annual checkpoint state. That study will add
recovery-certified magnitude-duration envelopes without mixing results from
the superseded independent-day formulation. Further work should incorporate
weather-varying cooling performance, storage degradation, network constraints,
service revenues and factorial interactions between resources.

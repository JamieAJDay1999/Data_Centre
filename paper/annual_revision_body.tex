\section{Methodology}\label{sec:methodology}

\subsection{Modelling scope and annual workflow}

The data centre is represented as an aggregate 1 MW facility because the
quantity of interest is grid-facing flexibility rather than server-level
placement. The retained states are those that constrain service delivery:
deadline-indexed computational work, UPS and TES energy, four facility
temperatures and total grid import. This abstraction preserves the coupling
between computation, electrical storage and cooling while remaining tractable
for a year of repeated mixed-integer linear programmes (MILPs).

The model uses 15-minute intervals. Each local calendar day is the committed
core of one solve and is followed by a 12-interval (three-hour) look-ahead.
Consequently, daylight-saving days contain 92 or 100 core intervals and normal
days contain 96. Only the core contributes to reported calendar-year cost; the
look-ahead exposes every flexible-work deadline and establishes a feasible
continuation. At the end of day $d$, the vector
\begin{equation}
 \boldsymbol{x}_{d}^{\mathrm{end}}=
 (E^{\mathrm{UPS}},E^{\mathrm{TES}},T_{\mathrm{IT}},T_{\mathrm{R}},
 T_{\mathrm{CA}},T_{\mathrm{HA}})
\end{equation}
is imposed as the initial state of day $d+1$. Outstanding workload is passed
as cohorts retaining its original arrival time and absolute deadline.

\subsection{Input data and workload construction}

The price signal is the actual signed 2025 Intermittent Market Reference Price
(IMRP), expanded from hourly observations to quarter-hour intervals. Negative
prices are retained. The workload is a stylised local-clock profile synthesised
from the utilisation and flexible-share ranges reported for Google and Alibaba
traces and related aggregate workload studies
\cite{6779441,Cao2022ApEn,Misaghian2023TIA,Xu2023SPIE,Fu2020ApEn,Liu2012PER}.
Reported tabular values were transcribed and graphical profiles digitised; the
hourly minima, maxima and means were compared, after which a representative
profile inside the observed envelope was selected while preserving the source
balance between inflexible and flexible demand. The resulting trace is
therefore a literature-synthesised modelling assumption, not a measurement of
a particular facility. It repeats in local clock time throughout the year.

At each arrival interval, flexible demand is divided equally among four delay
classes with maximum deferrals of 0.5, 1, 2 and 3 hours. If $a_c$ is the
CPU-hours arriving in cohort $c$, $u_{c,t}$ its scheduled utilisation and
$\mathcal W_c$ its feasible execution window, then
\begin{align}
 \sum_{t\in\mathcal W_c}u_{c,t}\Delta t &= a_c,\\
 u_t^{\mathrm{inflex}}+\sum_c u_{c,t} &\leq 1,
 \qquad u_{c,t}\geq0. \label{eq:workload}
\end{align}
Any balance whose deadline lies beyond the committed day is carried forward.
The complete three-hour look-ahead is as long as the maximum permitted delay,
so every cohort created in the core has a visible deadline.

Aggregate IT electrical power follows
\begin{equation}
 P_t^{\mathrm{IT}}=P_{\mathrm{idle}}+
 (P_{\max}-P_{\mathrm{idle}})u_t^{1.32},
 \label{eq:it-power}
\end{equation}
where $P_{\mathrm{idle}}=166.7$ kW and $P_{\max}=1000$ kW. A four-segment
non-uniform DLOG piecewise-linear representation is used in the MILP. Its
maximum error over $0\leq u\leq1$ is 6.241 kW (0.624\% of rated IT power).

\subsection{UPS, TES and thermal dynamics}

The central UPS has 600 kWh capacity, a 50\% minimum state of charge, charge
and discharge efficiencies of 0.82 and 0.92, and a 270 kW charging limit. Its
recursion is
\begin{equation}
 E^{\mathrm{UPS}}_{t+1}=E^{\mathrm{UPS}}_t+
 \eta_{\mathrm{ch}}P^{\mathrm{UPS}}_{\mathrm{ch},t}\Delta t-
 \frac{P^{\mathrm{UPS}}_{\mathrm{dis},t}}{\eta_{\mathrm{dis}}}\Delta t.
 \label{eq:ups}
\end{equation}
A binary mode variable prevents simultaneous charge and discharge. UPS
discharge supplies IT behind the meter, so
$P^{\mathrm{grid,IT}}_t=P^{\mathrm{IT}}_t-P^{\mathrm{UPS}}_{\mathrm{dis},t}$.

The 1000 kWh-th TES has 300 kW-th charge and discharge limits and 0.9
efficiencies in both directions:
\begin{equation}
 E^{\mathrm{TES}}_{t+1}=E^{\mathrm{TES}}_t+
 \left(\eta^{\mathrm{TES}}_{\mathrm{ch}}Q^{\mathrm{TES}}_{\mathrm{ch},t}
 -\frac{Q^{\mathrm{TES}}_{\mathrm{dis},t}}
 {\eta^{\mathrm{TES}}_{\mathrm{dis}}}\right)\Delta t. \label{eq:tes}
\end{equation}
Charging and discharging are mutually exclusive. The chiller coefficient of
performance is 5 and its total electrical input is limited to 400 kW:
\begin{align}
 Q^{\mathrm{cool}}_t&=\mathrm{COP}\,P^{\mathrm{ch,CRAC}}_t+
 Q^{\mathrm{TES}}_{\mathrm{dis},t},\\
 Q^{\mathrm{TES}}_{\mathrm{ch},t}&=\mathrm{COP}\,
 P^{\mathrm{ch,TES}}_t.
\end{align}

The four-node IT, rack, cold-aisle and hot-aisle model uses the heat capacities,
air flow and conductances documented in the facility model. Backward Euler is
used because the configured air-flow time constants make a 15-minute explicit
update unstable. For example, the IT and cold-aisle balances are
\begin{align}
 T^{\mathrm{IT}}_{t+1}&=T^{\mathrm{IT}}_t+\frac{\Delta t_s}{C_{\mathrm{IT}}}
 \left[1000P^{\mathrm{IT}}_t-G_{\mathrm{conv}}
 (T^{\mathrm{IT}}_{t+1}-T^{\mathrm{R}}_{t+1})\right],\\
 T^{\mathrm{CA}}_{t+1}&=T^{\mathrm{CA}}_t+\frac{\Delta t_s}{C_{\mathrm{CA}}}
 \left[\dot m\kappa c_p(T^{\mathrm{in}}_t-T^{\mathrm{CA}}_{t+1})\right.\notag\\
 &\qquad\left.-G_{\mathrm{cold}}
 (T^{\mathrm{CA}}_{t+1}-T^{\mathrm{out}})\right].
\end{align}
The rack and hot-aisle balances use the same implicit end states. Cold-aisle
temperature is constrained to 18--23$^{\circ}$C, outdoor temperature is fixed
at 22$^{\circ}$C, and
\begin{equation}
 Q^{\mathrm{cool}}_t\geq1000P^{\mathrm{IT}}_t
 \label{eq:cooling-rule}
\end{equation}
prevents unvalidated accumulation of IT heat from being treated as a free
flexibility resource.

\subsection{Grid balance, objective and annual cases}

With a constant 53.095 kW auxiliary load, grid import is
\begin{equation}
 P^{\mathrm{grid}}_t=P^{\mathrm{IT}}_t-P^{\mathrm{UPS}}_{\mathrm{dis},t}
 +P^{\mathrm{UPS}}_{\mathrm{ch},t}+P^{\mathrm{ch,CRAC}}_t
 +P^{\mathrm{ch,TES}}_t+P^{\mathrm{aux}}. \label{eq:grid}
\end{equation}
The objective minimises signed energy settlement cost across the core and
look-ahead,
\begin{equation}
 \min \sum_t \pi_tP^{\mathrm{grid}}_t\Delta t/1000, \label{eq:objective}
\end{equation}
without storage degradation, standing losses, terminal-energy credit or
network charges. The first annual interval starts with 600 kWh in the UPS,
500 kWh-th in TES and temperatures $(28.5,26,20,27)^{\circ}$C; no artificial
daily cyclic condition is imposed.

The benchmark processes work on arrival and disables UPS and TES dispatch,
while retaining cost-optimised cooling under the same thermal limits. The
optimised case enables all three resources. Full-year one-at-a-time endpoints
set flexible-workload share, UPS capacity and TES capacity to $0.5\times$ and
$1.5\times$ their central values, preserving storage power ratings and initial
energy fractions. Twelve representative weeks supply only the intermediate
$0.75\times$ and $1.25\times$ response shape. Pyomo and HiGHS solve each MILP
with a requested 0.1\% relative gap, a 300 s daily limit and a normally accepted
gap of 1\%.
The reported runs used Python 3.12.0, Pyomo 6.10.1 and HiGHS 1.15.1 on
Windows 11 with an eight-core AMD Ryzen 7 9800X3D processor.

\subsection{Representative-day flexibility assessment}\label{sec:scenario 3}

A representative normal-length day is selected objectively. For every eligible
day, price mean, dispersion and extremes; negative-price share; mean and peak
grid import; CPU utilisation; cost; and opening UPS, TES and cold-aisle states
are standardised against the annual distribution. The smallest root-mean-square
standardised distance selects 22 September 2025.

The exact 96-interval annual trajectory and the following 12 intervals are
loaded from the frozen annual result. For a request starting at $t_0$, the
stored aggregate CPU path is fixed before the event and the six physical states
at $t_0$ are pinned to their stored values. Deadline-level cohort allocation is
reconstructed consistently with that aggregate path because the original
annual export retained aggregate CPU rather than every intra-day cohort
decision. Thus no aggregate workload or physical-state preconditioning is
credited to the event.

For requested deviation $\Delta P$, the event constraint is
\begin{equation}
 \left|P^{\mathrm{grid}}_t-
 (P^{\mathrm{grid,base}}_t+\Delta P)\right|\leq0.1\ \mathrm{kW}.
 \label{eq:flex-target}
\end{equation}
Negative $\Delta P$ denotes upward flexibility (reduced import) and positive
$\Delta P$ denotes downward flexibility (increased import). Start times are
sampled every hour. Negative requests span -500 to -100 kW in 50 kW increments;
positive requests are 25, 50 and 75 kW. The search
builds a certified duration in one-hour increments, then refines the first
failed interval to 15-minute resolution, up to 12 hours or the end of the local
day. The first longer duration is also solved as a boundary check.
For a feasible request, horizon-terminal UPS and TES energy must be no worse
than the annual continuation and each terminal temperature must lie within
0.05$^{\circ}$C of it. Equation~\eqref{eq:objective} guides each trial towards
a least-cost feasible dispatch. A 60 s screening limit is used per solve; a
timeout without an incumbent is not classified as infeasibility. If the next
15-minute increment remains unresolved, the accepted duration is retained as
a certified lower bound and marked ``+'' in the heat map. Selected
component-decomposition cases permit an extended boundary check.

For interpretation, the realised grid deviation is decomposed additively as
\begin{equation}
 \begin{split}
 \Delta P^{\mathrm{grid}}={}&\Delta P^{\mathrm{IT}}+
 \Delta P^{\mathrm{ch,CRAC}}+\Delta P^{\mathrm{ch,TES}}\\
 &+\Delta(P^{\mathrm{UPS}}_{\mathrm{ch}}-P^{\mathrm{UPS}}_{\mathrm{dis}}).
 \end{split}
 \label{eq:component-decomposition}
\end{equation}
Every event is audited for target tracking, recovery, workload conservation,
objective reconciliation, mode exclusivity and component closure.

\section{Results and Discussion}\label{sec:results}

\subsection{Central annual result and cost composition}

The workload-at-arrival benchmark costs GBP~550,503.59 in 2025, whereas
integrated optimisation costs GBP~522,707.23. The saving is GBP~27,796.37, or
5.049\%. Table~\ref{tab:annual-endpoints} reports all completed full-year
endpoints.

\input{generated/annual_endpoint_table.tex}

The optimised case imports 6.794 GWh compared with 6.718 GWh in the benchmark.
The saving therefore comes from moving load to cheaper signed-price intervals,
not from reducing annual energy. Peak import rises from 974.9 to 1681.9 kW as
storage charging and deferred-work recovery concentrate consumption. This is
an energy-market result; capacity tariffs or a tighter grid constraint would
change the dispatch.

Table~\ref{tab:annual-components} reconciles exactly to the settlement totals.
Relative to the benchmark, time-shifted IT demand reduces signed cost by
GBP~15,239 and CRAC operation by GBP~14,085. TES charging adds GBP~6,905,
whereas the net UPS grid effect contributes a GBP~5,377 reduction. The fixed
auxiliary contribution is unchanged. These are settlement contributions, not
stand-alone asset profits: each value depends on the jointly optimised dispatch.

\subsection{Sensitivity and asset ranking}

Flexible-workload share is the dominant tested parameter. Halving it reduces
the annual saving to 3.522\%, while increasing it by half raises the saving to
6.257\%. UPS capacity ranks second, spanning 4.565--5.501\%, and TES capacity
third, spanning 4.800--5.167\%. The frozen ranking is therefore flexible
workload, UPS energy capacity and TES energy capacity.

Figure~\ref{fig:flexibility-heatmap} shows a strongly time-dependent and

does not imply a monotonic
duration at every start because the request is an exact signed grid target in a
mixed-integer system: it changes which combinations of delayed work, discrete
storage modes and thermal recovery are reachable.

Figures~\ref{fig:upward-components} and \ref{fig:downward-components} show that
the same external request can be delivered by different internal portfolios.
Reduced import combines IT deferral, lower CRAC/TES charging demand and UPS
discharge, followed by compensating changes within the event and recovery
horizon. Increased import is obtained mainly by advancing feasible work and
raising its associated CRAC demand, with TES charging contributing when thermal
headroom is available. The component bars close to the requested grid deviation
to within $4.4\times10^{-11}$ kW; the largest event tracking error is 0.1 kW and
the largest terminal-temperature error is 0.05$^{\circ}$C.

\subsection{Continuity, solver quality and terminal treatment}

Every annual chain passes timestamp coverage, state hand-off, workload
conservation, charge/discharge exclusivity and settlement reconciliation. The
few time-limited daily solves retain feasible incumbents. The largest exceptional
relative gap occurs in the low-flexibility case on 25 May: a 900 s rerun leaves
a 5.652\% relative gap because its negative objective is close to zero, but the
absolute incumbent--bound width is only GBP~0.102. Its committed cost changes
by about GBP~0.002, its downstream state is unchanged and all physical audits
pass; it is retained with this qualification.

At midnight after 31 December, 0.642875 CPU-hours remain within deadline and
are completed in the actual 1 January 2026 look-ahead. Calendar-year reporting
includes only 2025 core intervals; look-ahead cost is a feasibility certificate.
A continuation-value test spanning the median to the maximum observed 1 January
price changes annual cost by at most 0.000381\%. This supports the zero-value
central convention while exposing its consequence.

The 288-cell flexibility grid uses a uniform hourly start-time mesh. All event
dispatches meet the accepted gap and all physical audits pass. Exact duration
boundaries are established for 283 cells. Five cells retain certified lower
bounds because the next 15-minute increment did not resolve within the 60 s
screening limit; these are marked explicitly rather than treating a timeout as
infeasibility.

\subsection{Scope and limitations}

This is a stylised operating experiment, not a forecast for a particular
facility. Ambient temperature, COP and the repeated local-clock workload shape
are fixed; storage ageing, standing losses, network tariffs and service revenue
are omitted. The exact-target event formulation is intentionally conservative
and does not model a market tolerance band beyond 0.1 kW. The representative
day demonstrates state-consistent duration capability but is not an annual
availability distribution. Finally, component contributions are dispatch
dependent and should not be interpreted as independently additive asset values.

\section{Conclusion}

Linked rolling optimisation across 2025 reduces modelled electricity cost from
GBP~550,503.59 to GBP~522,707.23, a saving of 5.049\%, while preserving physical
and workload continuity through every local day and daylight-saving transition.
Full-year endpoints identify flexible workload as the most influential tested
resource, followed by UPS and TES energy capacity. The additive annual cost
table shows how IT rescheduling and CRAC timing produce most of the gross
reduction, while TES and UPS contributions reflect their joint dispatch.

Starting from the same frozen annual trajectory, the representative-day study
converts this economic flexibility into a recovery-certified grid-service
envelope. Increased import reaches 9.75 hours at +25 kW, while reduced import
reaches 5.25 hours at -100 and -150 kW. Component decompositions
demonstrate that equivalent grid responses can use materially different mixes
of workload, cooling, TES and UPS flexibility. This dependence on start time,
direction and internal state is precisely why independent reset-day estimates
are insufficient for credible flexibility offers.

Future work should extend the linked formulation to weather-varying cooling
performance, battery degradation, network constraints, service revenues,
multi-day availability statistics and factorial interactions among resources.

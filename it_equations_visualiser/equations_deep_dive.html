Eq. (1)

Constant Computational Demand Principle

Foundational Concept
$$R = u(t) \times \Delta T$$

The core axiom of workload flexibility: an IT job requires a total accumulation of processor occupancy over time. This demand can be executed either at high utilisation over a short duration or low utilisation over a long duration, without altering the underlying volume of computational work.

Term Role & Units Detailed Mathematical & Physical Definition
R CPU-hours Scalar ≥ 0 Total Computational Demand: The aggregate volume of processing required to complete the job. For example, 1.0 CPU-hour can be satisfied by 100% CPU utilisation for 1 hour, or 50% CPU utilisation for 2 hours.
u(t) Fraction [0, 1] 0.0 ≤ u(t) ≤ u_max Allocated CPU Utilisation: The average fraction of server processor cores occupied by the workload during time interval \(t\).
\Delta T Hours \Delta T > 0 Execution Duration: The continuous or aggregated time over which processor resources are dedicated to the job.
Eq. (2)

Server Power Consumption Model

Component Physics
$$P^{\mathrm{IT}}(t) = P^{\mathrm{IT}}_{\mathrm{idle}} + \left(P^{\mathrm{IT}}_{\mathrm{max}} - P^{\mathrm{IT}}_{\mathrm{idle}}\right) \times u(t)^{1.32}$$

Empirical non-linear power curve relating processor activity to total electrical power draw of the server chassis. Derived from literature benchmarks (Dayarathna et al.), capturing baseline idle power plus super-linear dynamic power scaling.

Term Role & Units Detailed Mathematical & Physical Definition
P^{\mathrm{IT}}(t) kW P_idle ≤ P(t) ≤ P_max Total Instantaneous IT Power: Electrical power drawn by the server hardware from the electrical distribution unit at interval \(t\).
P^{\mathrm{IT}}_{\mathrm{idle}} kW Constant = 200 kW Idle Power Draw: Power consumed when servers are powered on with 0% CPU utilisation. Represents motherboard circuitry, static leakage currents, memory refresh, power supply conversion losses, and minimum fan speeds.
P^{\mathrm{IT}}_{\mathrm{max}} kW Constant = 1,000 kW (1 MW) Peak Rated IT Power: Maximum facility electrical draw when processor cores are operating at 100% capacity (\(u=1.0\)).
u(t)^{1.32} Dimensionless Exponent = 1.32 Non-linear Dynamic Scaling Exponent: Captures dynamic CMOS switching losses and voltage/frequency scaling under load. Because \(1.32 > 1\), the curve is slightly convex: running two servers at 50% utilisation consumes less dynamic power than running one at 100% and leaving one idle.
Eq. (3)

Optimised IT Electrical Power Consumption

Substantive Correction
$$P^{\mathrm{IT}}_{\mathrm{opt}}(s) = \widehat f\!\left(u^{\mathrm{inflex}}(s) + \sum_{(t,k) \in \mathcal A_d :\, s \in W_{t,k}} u(t,k,s)\right), \quad s \in T^{\mathrm{ext}}$$

Substantive Physical Correction & Approximation Operator

In Paper 1, extension power was calculated by subtracting baseline power: \(P_{\text{opt}} = f(u) - P_{\text{base}}\). This made \(P_{\text{opt}}\) an incremental delta rather than facility power. Paper 2 restores full physical electrical demand across all slots \(s \in T^{\text{ext}}\) and writes \(\widehat{f}\) to denote the exact 4-segment piecewise linear interpolation solved by the MILP.
Term Role & Units Detailed Mathematical & Physical Definition
P^{\mathrm{IT}}_{\mathrm{opt}}(s) kW 200 kW ≤ P ≤ 1,000 kW Full Optimised IT Physical Power Demand: The true electrical power consumed by all servers at quarter-hour interval \(s\). Directly feeds electrical converter supply (\(P_{\text{grid}} + P_{\text{ups}}\)) and thermal heat rejection (\(Q^{\text{IT}}\)).
\widehat{f}(\cdot) Function: [0, 1] → [200, 1000] Piecewise Linear Continuous Piecewise-Linear Solver Approximation: A 4-segment linear interpolation of \(P(u) = 200 + 800 u^{1.32}\) implemented via Pyomo DLOG/SOS2 constraints. Uses breakpoints \(b_i = (i/4)^{1.5} \in [0.0, 0.125, 0.354, 0.650, 1.0]\). Peak approximation error is capped at 6.241 kW (0.624%).
u^{\mathrm{inflex}}(s) Fraction [0, 1] Exogenous Input Profile Inflexible Workload Utilisation: The baseline CPU utilisation of mission-critical or non-shiftable computational jobs that must execute strictly in slot \(s\).
\mathcal{A}_d Set of Tuples (t, k) Active Cohort Ledger Active Horizon Cohorts: The set of all workload cohorts present during daily solve \(d\). Contains two types of jobs: (1) unfinished cohorts carried forward from earlier days, and (2) new workload arrivals arriving throughout the extended horizon \(T_d^{\mathrm{ext}}\).
W_{t,k} Set of Slot Indices W_{t,k} = [t, t + D_k] \cap T^{\mathrm{ext}} Permitted Execution Window: The discrete set of 15-minute timeslots between arrival \(t\) and deadline \(t + D_k\) during which cohort \((t,k)\) is legally allowed to execute.
u(t,k,s) Fraction [0, 1] Continuous Decision Variable Cohort CPU Allocation Variable: The portion of server processor utilisation allocated to cohort \((t,k)\) to run during execution slot \(s\).
T^{\mathrm{ext}} Set of Intervals |T^{\mathrm{ext}}| = 108 slots (27 h) Extended Daily Horizon: Comprises the 96 quarter-hour slots of the local day core (\(T_d\)) plus 12 quarter-hour look-ahead slots (\(T^{\mathrm{ext}} \setminus T_d\)).
Eq. (4)

CPU Utilisation Allocation Bounds

Multi-Day Extension
$$0 \le u(t,k,s) \le u_{\max}, \quad (t,k) \in \mathcal A_d, \ s \in W_{t,k}$$

Enforces physical processor allocation boundaries for each individual cohort. Replaces the single-day arrival index (\(t \in T, k \in K\)) with \((t,k) \in \mathcal{A}_d\) to ensure carried-over jobs are bound by the same operational limits.

Term Role & Units Detailed Mathematical & Physical Definition
0 Lower Bound Non-negativity Non-Negativity Constraint: Prevents negative processor utilisation (servers cannot "un-process" work or generate computational negative work).
u_{\max} Fraction = 1.0 Physical Upper Limit Maximum Server Capacity: The peak normalised processor capacity of the representative facility server (1.0 = 100% capacity).
s \in W_{t,k} Index Qualifier Valid Window Restricts decision variables to slots inside the permitted execution window. Decision variables outside \(W_{t,k}\) are fixed to zero by Equation (8).
Eq. (5)

Baseline IT Power Benchmark

Linearisation Consistency
$$P^{\mathrm{IT}}_{\mathrm{base}}(s) = \widehat f\bigl(u^{\mathrm{inflex}}(s) + u^{\mathrm{flex,base}}(s)\bigr)$$

Defines the benchmark power consumption profile of the facility in the absence of workload shifting. Evaluates the exact same piecewise linear interpolation \(\widehat{f}\) used in Equation (3).

Term Role & Units Detailed Mathematical & Physical Definition
P^{\mathrm{IT}}_{\mathrm{base}}(s) kW Benchmark Profile Unoptimised Electrical Power: The facility power draw when all flexible workloads execute immediately upon arrival without temporal shifting.
u^{\mathrm{flex,base}}(s) Fraction [0, 1] Arrival Workload Flexible Arrival Utilisation: The CPU utilisation that would be demanded if all shiftable jobs arriving at interval \(s\) were processed immediately at arrival.
Eqs. (6–7)

Baseline and Optimised IT Electrical Energy

Energy Conversion
$$E^{\mathrm{IT}}_{\mathrm{base}}(t) = \Delta t \cdot P^{\mathrm{IT}}_{\mathrm{base}}(t), \quad E^{\mathrm{IT}}_{\mathrm{opt}}(t) = \Delta t \times P^{\mathrm{IT}}_{\mathrm{opt}}(t), \quad \forall t \in T^{\text{ext}}$$
Term Role & Units Detailed Mathematical & Physical Definition
E^{\mathrm{IT}}_{\mathrm{opt}}(t) kWh Physical Energy Optimised Electrical Energy: Real physical kilowatt-hours consumed across 15 minutes. In Paper 2, because \(P^{\mathrm{IT}}_{\mathrm{opt}}\) is true physical power, this is genuine facility energy throughout the entire 27-hour solve.
\Delta t Hours = 0.25 Fixed Step Time Step Duration: 15 minutes (\(0.25\) hours), matching Great Britain electricity settlement periods and market reference pricing intervals.
Eq. (8)

Temporal Feasibility & SLA Window Restriction

Causality & SLA
$$u(t,k,s) = 0 \quad \text{if } s < t \text{ or } s > t + D_k, \quad (t,k) \in \mathcal A_d$$
Term Role & Units Detailed Mathematical & Physical Definition
s < t Logical Condition Pre-arrival Interval Temporal Causality Constraint: A job cannot be scheduled or executed before it has physically arrived in the system.
s > t + D_k Logical Condition Post-deadline Interval Maximum Deferral Deadline: Execution is forbidden after interval start \(t + D_k\). Guarantees that flexible workloads strictly respect their delay tolerance.
D_k Hours D \in \{0.5, 1.0, 2.0, 3.0\} Tranche Delay Allowance: The maximum allowable deferral duration for tranche \(k\): Tranche 1 allows up to 30 min (2 slots), Tranche 2 up to 1 h (4 slots), Tranche 3 up to 2 h (8 slots), and Tranche 4 up to 3 h (12 slots).
Eq. (9)

Tranche Share Partitioning

Partition Conservation
$$\sum_{k \in K} \alpha_{t,k} = 1 \quad \text{for each new arrival } t$$
Term Role & Units Detailed Mathematical & Physical Definition
\alpha_{t,k} Fraction [0, 1] Exogenous Input Table Tranche Partition Share: The fraction of total flexible arrival demand at timeslot \(t\) allocated to tranche \(k\). Listed in Table 2 of the paper (time-of-day varying).
\text{new arrival } t Domain Condition Fresh Arrivals Only Carried Cohort Clarification: Workload carried over from yesterday already has its tranche fixed at original arrival. This condition specifies that \(\alpha\) applies only when partitioning brand new arrivals.
Eq. (10)

Work Completion & Boundary Continuity Constraint

Multi-Day Extension (Crucial)
$$\sum_{s \in W_{t,k}} u(t,k,s)\Delta t \begin{cases} = A_{t,k,d}, & t + D_k \le L_d, \\[6pt] \le A_{t,k,d}, & t + D_k > L_d \end{cases}$$

Why the Split (= vs ≤) is Mathematically Required

In continuous rolling annual operation, jobs arriving in the late evening or during the look-ahead have valid legal deadlines extending into the next day. Enforcing strict equality (\(=\)) would force the solver to execute 100% of the job within today's solve, prematurely truncating the user's allowable delay or causing MILP solver infeasibility. The inequality (\(\le\)) permits partial processing, and residual unexecuted work transfers cleanly into Day \(d+1\).
Term Role & Units Detailed Mathematical & Physical Definition
\sum_{s \in W_{t,k}} u(t,k,s)\Delta t CPU-hours Accumulated Work Accumulated Execution: Total computational work delivered to cohort \((t,k)\) across all eligible slots in the current 27-hour horizon.
A_{t,k,d} CPU-hours Available Demand Currently Available Workload: For a freshly arriving cohort, \(A_{t,k,d} = R_t \alpha_{t,k}\). For a cohort carried over from yesterday, \(A_{t,k,d}\) is the residual uncompleted work handed off from Day \(d-1\).
t + D_k \le L_d Condition: Equality (=) Inside Horizon Deadline Complete Execution Required: If the job's final legal interval start \(t + D_k\) falls inside or at the horizon boundary \(L_d\), all available work must be completely finished before the solve terminates.
t + D_k > L_d Condition: Inequality (≤) Crossing Horizon Deadline Partial Execution Permitted: If the job's deadline extends beyond the current solve horizon, the solver may process anywhere between 0% and 100% of available work today. The remaining work carries forward to tomorrow without penalty.
L_d Timestamp / Slot L_d = 26.75 h (Slot 107) Final Horizon Interval Start: The opening timestamp of the last 15-minute slot in the 27-hour solve (02:45 UTC on the following day).
Eq. (11)

Per-Time-Slot Server CPU Capacity Bound

Capacity Constraint
$$u^{\mathrm{inflex}}(s) + \sum_{(t,k) \in \mathcal A_d :\, s \in W_{t,k}} u(t,k,s) \le u_{\max}, \quad s \in T^{\mathrm{ext}}$$

Enforces that the aggregate processor load at execution interval \(s\) (inflexible baseline plus all executing flexible cohorts) never exceeds total server processor capacity (\(u_{\max} = 1.0\)).

Term Role & Units Detailed Mathematical & Physical Definition
u^{\mathrm{inflex}}(s) Fraction [0, 1] Inflexible Core Load Dedicated CPU capacity reserved for non-shiftable computational services during interval \(s\).
\sum_{(t,k) \in \mathcal{A}_d : s \in W_{t,k}} u(t,k,s) Fraction [0, 1] Sum of Flexible Jobs Total CPU utilisation demanded by all flexible cohorts scheduled to execute during interval \(s\). Includes jobs arriving today and carried jobs from yesterday.
u_{\max} 1.0 (100%) Hardware Capacity Physical capacity limit of the server processor cores.
Handoff Eq.

Cohort Residual Workload Handoff

Annual Chaining Engine
$$A_{t,k,d+1} = A_{t,k,d} - \sum_{s \in T_d \cap W_{t,k}} u(t,k,s)\Delta t$$

Found in annual_operation.tex (Line 6, Eq. 6)

This equation defines the exact operational rule that links consecutive daily solves across the 365-day continuous year. It prevents double-counting by subtracting only execution that occurred during the committed 24-hour core day \(T_d\).
Term Role & Units Detailed Mathematical & Physical Definition
A_{t,k,d+1} CPU-hours Opening Balance Day d+1 Remaining Unexecuted Work: The exact workload residual passed into Day \(d+1\) for cohort \((t,k)\).
A_{t,k,d} CPU-hours Opening Balance Day d The available computational demand at the start of Day \(d\).
s \in T_d \cap W_{t,k} Committed Subset Core Slots Only The Committed Deduction Filter: Execution is subtracted only if slot \(s\) falls within the committed local day core \(T_d\) (slots 0 to 95). Advisory or trial execution during the 3-hour look-ahead (\(s > 95\)) is discarded and re-optimised the next morning, ensuring work is never prematurely deducted or double-counted!
Cost Eq.

Annual Electricity Settlement Accounting

Annual Settlement
$$C^{(i)}_{\mathrm{year}} = \sum_{d=1}^{365} \sum_{t \in T_d} P^{\mathrm{Grid},(i)}(t) \pi(t) \Delta t / 1000$$
Term Role & Units Detailed Mathematical & Physical Definition
C^{(i)}_{\mathrm{year}} GBP (£) Annual Total Cost Annual Electricity Bill: Total facility expenditure on imported grid electricity over all 365 days of 2025. Evaluated for Scenario 1 (\(i=1\), benchmark) and Scenario 2 (\(i=2\), optimised).
\sum_{d=1}^{365} \sum_{t \in T_d} Summation 35,040 quarter-hours Single-Settlement Guarantee: Sums each quarter-hour interval exactly once across 2025 (accounting for 92, 96, and 100 slots on daylight-saving transitions). Excludes look-ahead intervals until their day becomes committed.
P^{\mathrm{Grid},(i)}(t) kW P ≥ 0 Total Grid Electrical Import: Power imported from the utility grid to supply IT servers, cooling chillers, auxiliary devices (\(P^{\mathrm{Grid}}_{\mathrm{OD}}\)), and UPS battery charging.
\pi(t) GBP / MWh Can be negative Day-Ahead Electricity Price: The 2025 Great Britain day-ahead Intermittent Market Reference Price (IMRP) published by LCCC.
1 / 1000 Conversion kW to MW Converts electrical power in kW to MW to match the £/MWh wholesale tariff rate.

UPS Battery State Chaining Across Days

Storage Arbitrage Continuity
$$E^{\mathrm{UPS}}_{d+1,\mathrm{start}} = E^{\mathrm{UPS}}_{d,\mathrm{core\ end}}, \quad E^{\mathrm{UPS}}_{1,\mathrm{start}} = E^{\mathrm{UPS}}_{\mathrm{base}}$$
Term Role & Units Detailed Mathematical & Physical Definition
E^{\mathrm{UPS}}_{d+1,\mathrm{start}} kWh Opening Energy Balance The initial state of charge of the UPS battery system at 00:00 UTC on Day \(d+1\).
E^{\mathrm{UPS}}_{d,\mathrm{core\ end}} kWh Committed Closing State The actual battery energy at the end of the committed 24-hour core day \(d\) (slot 96). Eliminates the artificial single-day constraint (\(E_{\text{start}} = E_{\text{end}} = 50\%\)) used in Paper 1.
E^{\mathrm{UPS}}_{\mathrm{base}} kWh = 600 Nominal Capacity Initial baseline capacity of the battery pack. A 50% lower operational reserve (300 kWh) is preserved at all times for emergency power reliability.

Eqs. (675–676)

Scenario 3 Flexibility Cohort Window Tracking

Flexibility SLA Integrity
$$W^{\mathrm{event}}_{t,k} = \bigl\{s : \max(t, t_0) \le s \le t + D_k\bigr\} \cap T^{\mathrm{event}}, \quad (t,k) \in \mathcal A_{t_0}$$

During demand response / grid balancing events starting at \(t_0\), Paper 2 maintains exact individual arrival/tranche cohorts \((t,k) \in \mathcal{A}_{t_0}\). Rather than discarding tranches, it strictly evaluates remaining allowable deferral.

Term Role & Units Detailed Mathematical & Physical Definition
W^{\mathrm{event}}_{t,k} Set of Slots Certified Active Slots The valid execution window available to cohort \((t,k)\) during the flexibility event.
t_0 Slot Index Event Start Time The exact quarter-hour interval at which the grid operator requests an upward or downward power deviation.
\max(t, t_0) Lower Bound Causality & Event Start Ensures that execution cannot occur before event start \(t_0\) or before the job's actual arrival \(t\).
t + D_k Upper Bound Immutable SLA Deadline Prevents Artificial Allowance Resets: If a job arriving at 10:00 with a 3-hour delay has already been deferred until 12:00 when an event begins at \(t_0 = 12:00\), its deadline remains strictly 13:00 (1 hour remaining). It cannot receive a fresh 3-hour deferral allowance.
